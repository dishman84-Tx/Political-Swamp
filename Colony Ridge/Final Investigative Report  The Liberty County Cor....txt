Part II: Analysis of Additional Violations and Systemic Corruption
The federal and state lawsuits focus primarily on consumer harm. However, a deeper investigation reveals a pattern of systemic corruption, conflicts of interest, and additional violations that created the environment for the alleged fraud to occur with minimal oversight.
A. The Legislative Framework: Engineering an Environment for Exploitation
The rapid, unchecked growth of Colony Ridge was enabled by a series of state-level legislative actions. These bills, sponsored by legislators with documented ties to the real estate, development, and engineering industries, created a permissive environment for developer-controlled special districts, granting them significant public power while simultaneously eroding local democratic oversight.

E. MMD & MUD Board Governance: Developer Self-Regulation
The Liberty County MMD and various MUD boards are documented as being controlled by developers and their affiliates. This structure facilitates direct self-dealing. According to the MMD's own June 2024 audit, the district owes $15.4 million to Colony Ridge Development, a clear example of the developer reimbursement model in practice. This is exemplified by the $22.5 million in contracts awarded by the MMD board to Liberty Paving, a company in which former MMD president Trey Harris had a direct financial interest. The MUD 15 election, which authorized $88 million in debt with only two votes cast, further demonstrates the manipulation of these public entities for private gain. Similarly, Liberty County MUD No. 12 (created by SB 2580 in 2023) was granted broad taxing, bond-issuing, and eminent domain powers over 764 acres, with provisions enabling it to be divided into multiple smaller developer-controlled districts. MMD No. 2 was created by SB 2594 in 2023 to expand this framework into 236 acres in the city limits and ETJ of Cleveland.
F. The Mechanics of Malfeasance: Predatory Practices and Environmental Negligence
Predatory Lending & "Foreclosure Mill" Model: The DOJ and Texas AG lawsuits allege a systemic "bait-and-switch" scheme targeting vulnerable, often non-English-speaking buyers with predatory, high-interest loans for land lacking basic infrastructure. The business model relies on an exceptionally high foreclosure rate—reportedly 50 times the national average—allowing the developer to reacquire and resell properties at a profit.
Documented Environmental Violations: The developer's practices have been the subject of formal state-level reprimands. As early as June 2021, the Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) finalized nine separate investigations into Colony Ridge's construction practices, finding violations in eight of the nine cases, including failure to control erosion and prevent sediment from polluting public waterways. These violations have led to widespread flooding in both Liberty and Harris counties.
G. Case Studies and Historical Parallels
Case Study: Mutual Protection in Liberty County (The Karbowski Case): In 2020, County Commissioner Bruce Karbowski—who is familially related to DA Jennifer Bergman (reportedly her uncle)—was indicted for nepotism. After taking office, DA Bergman's office took control of the case and secured his acquittal by presenting no evidence at trial. The presiding judge, Hon. Mark Morefield, was later publicly admonished by the State Commission on Judicial Conduct for his handling of the case, in which he admitted bias and prejudice partly based on "county rumor and speculation." This sequence, confirmed in official documents (RQ-0550-KP and CJC No. 23-0537), demonstrates a breakdown of impartial justice involving the DA's office and the judiciary.
Case Study: Selective Recusal (The David Whitmire Case): In April 2025, Pct. 3 County Commissioner David Whitmire was indicted for sexual assault, official oppression, and solicitation of prostitution. DA Jennifer Bergman's office recused itself, citing a financial conflict since the Commissioners Court controls her budget. While acknowledging this ethical boundary, critics pointed out her failure to recuse herself from land-use and developmental prosecutions where her family's engineering and surveying companies (Bergman Engineering) had direct business interests, such as in the FM 1008 corridor.
Case Study: Cracks in Law Enforcement Integrity (Jason Grindstaff & Fire Marshal William Hergemueller): In late 2024, Liberty County Fire Marshal William Hergemueller and two deputies were fired and faced charges of performing unauthorized inspections and theft. This was followed on July 3, 2026, by the surrender and arrest of former Liberty Police Officer Jason Grindstaff after a grand jury indictment for official oppression and indecent assault following a Texas Rangers investigation. These recurring arrests highlight systemic public integrity and regulatory failures within the county's enforcement arms.
Historical Parallel: The Trinity Savings & Loan Scandal (1980s): The current situation has deep historical roots. The Trinity Savings & Loan Association of Cleveland, TX, was at the center of a major FBI investigation during the S&L crisis for a massive criminal conspiracy involving fraudulent land flips and self-dealing, providing a historical precedent for complex, real estate-based financial malfeasance in the area.

IX. Conclusion: A Pattern of Systemic Corruption
The body of evidence detailed in this report points toward a coordinated, multi-year pattern of activity consistent with a potential criminal enterprise. The facts suggest a system in which legislative authority was captured, public governance structures were co-opted, and official duties were compromised to facilitate the private financial interests of a small, interconnected network of individuals. The mutually reinforcing actions, from the dismissal of criminal charges against family members to the suspicious timing of campaign donations and official acts, created an environment where the alleged large-scale consumer fraud at the heart of the DOJ and Texas AG lawsuits could occur with minimal local oversight. This warrants a comprehensive investigation by state and federal authorities to determine the full extent of the potential corruption and to restore public trust.
X. Investigative Recommendations
Investigate Potential Criminal Enterprise: A formal investigation into potential racketeering and organized criminal activity should be initiated, focusing on the coordinated actions of the individuals and entities within the identified networks.
Conduct a Forensic Audit of MMD #1 and MUD #15: A full financial audit is necessary to trace the flow of public funds, specifically focusing on the $22.5 million awarded to Liberty Paving, the $15.4 million owed to the developer, and the $88 million in debt approved by MUD 15.
Scrutinize Financial Transactions for Quid Pro Quo Corruption: An inquiry into the direct correlation between campaign contributions and subsequent official actions is necessary across the board, including donations to Governor Abbott, the Liberty County Sheriff, and the Walker County Sheriff (in the case of Trey Harris's early release).
Investigate Abuse of Office and Conflicts of Interest: A formal investigation into potential violations of the Texas Penal Code is warranted. This should include a full review of the DA's active law firm, her lawsuit against the CAD, the handling of the Karbowski nepotism case, and the operations of the County Clerk's office regarding election and property records.
Audit Liberty County Commissioners Court Records: A full review of the Commissioners Court minutes, agendas, and backup materials is required to identify all votes, contracts, and appointments related to the Colony Ridge development, MUDs, and associated infrastructure projects to determine the extent of the court's complicity or negligence.
