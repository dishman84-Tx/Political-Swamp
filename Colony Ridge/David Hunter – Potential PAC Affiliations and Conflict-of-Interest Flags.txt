David Hunter – Potential PAC Affiliations and
Conflict-of-Interest Flags
Official Filings and PAC Support
Independent PAC Expenditures:David Hunter received indirect campaign support from at least one
political  action  committee.  Notably,  a  Washington  D.C.-based  PAC  called  American  Values  First
spent money on  independent advertising for Hunter’s 2024 re-election. Texas Ethics Commission
records  show  this  PAC  paid  for  direct  mailers  “supporting  David  Hunter  for  Liberty  County
Constable, Precinct 5” ahead of the March 2024 primary. This PAC’s filings (treasurer Dustin
McIntyre  in  D.C.)  indicate  it  was  a  specific-purpose  committee,  ostensibly  tied  to  a  state
representative’s  campaign  but  actively  funding  local  constable  races.  These  independent
expenditures would  not appear on Hunter’s own campaign finance reports, potentially obscuring
outside support.
Campaign Finance Disclosures: Hunter’s official county campaign finance reports on file in Liberty
County show no large direct PAC contributions in his name (the reports reviewed were largely zero-
dollar reports) . The cover sheets include a section for “political contributions by committees,”
but no committee donations were listed, implying any PAC involvement was through independent
spending rather than direct contributions. For example, no  in-kind donations or PAC checks are
reflected in his January or May 2024 reports on record. This gap between PAC activity and Hunter’s
disclosures highlights a potential transparency issue: significant campaign support (like the mailers
from American Values First) occurred off his balance sheet.
Party and PAC Contributions: While no direct monetary contributions from local PACs are recorded
in Hunter’s filings, there is evidence of close ties to party organizations. Hunter’s expenditure records
(30-day pre-election report) show a payment to the Liberty County Republican Party, likely for
a  candidate  event  or  filing  fee.  Additionally,  a  local  Republican  women’s  PAC  –  Nancy  Hart
Republican Women PAC – has been actively involved in the Liberty County political scene. Hunter’s
campaign activities intersect with this group (e.g. attending their meetings and events), suggesting
he benefits from their support network even if formal contributions aren’t logged.

the County Judge, and a State Representative) for attending an event. Photo evidence: In group
photos from GOP gatherings, Hunter is often seen alongside other candidates, elected officials, and
supporters. One candidate filing day photo shows Hunter with a slate of Liberty County Republican
officials and candidates (taken at a party-hosted event). These appearances indicate Hunter is

News Mentions of Fundraising and Associations

Hunter (Pct. 5 Constable) alongside Sheriff Rader , several constables, and commissioners. Such

candidate filings, it underlines that Hunter’s campaign was not an isolated effort – it was supported
by a broader network of party-aligned donors and organizers. No specific  controversial donor is
named in these events, but attendees often include county contractors and influential community
members, creating opportunities for relationship-building.

Advanced Search Strategies for Political Auditing
To thoroughly audit Liberty County officials (including Hunter) for conflicts of interest, we recommend a
multi-pronged search and analysis approach. These tactics blend traditional open-source investigation with
role-specific corruption indicators:
Cross-Reference Donors with County Contracts: Compile lists of top campaign contributors from
finance reports and compare them against county vendor registries and contract awardees. This
helps flag if a company or individual donating to an official later received county business (or vice
versa). For example, if a constable’s donor is also a towing service or equipment supplier winning
precinct contracts, that relationship should be scrutinized for favoritism.
Public Records and Database Mining: Leverage state and county databases for any mention of the
official in lobbying registrations, ethics disclosures, or business ownership records. In Texas, check
Ethics Commission filings for any specific-purpose PACs or independent expenditures naming the
official (as we did with American Values First). Also search the official’s name in procurement
databases and transparency portals for financial links (e.g. reimbursements, property transactions
with donors).
Social Media & Photo Analysis: Perform targeted social media searches (Facebook, Instagram, local
forums) for tagged photos or posts of the official at events. Use image recognition or manual review
to identify notable figures in those photos (such as known lobbyists, developers, or PAC organizers).
In Hunter’s case, Facebook posts by groups like Nancy Hart R.W. PAC were illuminating. For each
official, catalog the events they attend and sponsors involved; this can reveal unreported gifts or soft
support. Tools like Face recognition or simply following the social networks of local political groups
can expose connections that aren’t in official filings.
News  Archives  &  Public  Documents: Search  local  news  outlets  (e.g.  Bluebonnet  News,  Liberty
Vindicator) for articles on campaign fundraisers, endorsement nights, and community functions. Pay
attention to who is mentioned as hosting or sponsoring these events. Sometimes a benign-sounding
community sponsor (like a “Friends of XYZ” group) is actually a PAC or a front for business interests.
Incorporate searches for terms like “under investigation”, “indicted”, or “fraud” alongside officials’
names  to  catch  any  emerging  scandals.  In  an  audit,  one  might  also  request  minutes  of

Role-Specific Red Flags: Tailor the search to the office in question:
For law enforcement officials (sheriffs, constables): Look for patterns of favor-trading – e.g. donations
from bail bond companies, wrecker/towing services, or firearm vendors, since those businesses
intersect with policing. Check for any unusually large gifts (like equipment “donated” to the
department by private firms) that might not show in finance reports. Also research whether the
official participates in asset forfeiture funds or police foundations that could be used to sidestep
campaign finance rules.
For commissioners and judges: Focus on developers, engineering firms, and legal contractors who
contribute to campaigns and then receive county contracts or favorable zoning decisions. In Liberty
County, large developments (like Colony Ridge) and infrastructure projects would be key areas – any
official with both regulatory power and campaign funding from the interested parties is a red flag.
For all officials: Apply public integrity models such as the  “follow the money” approach and the
Benford’s Law anomaly detection on reported finances. For instance, verify if expenditures claimed
(e.g. reimbursements or donations) match actual events. An election fraud screening might include
checking if any reported donors are straw donors (multiple contributions under different names
from the same source) or if any required disclosures (gifts, travel, nepotism forms) are missing. 
Data  Aggregation  for  Dashboards: Structure  the  findings  in  a  spreadsheet-friendly  format  for
analysis. Each potential affiliation or conflict can be logged as a row with fields like  Official Name, 
Affiliation/Donor,  Type of Connection,  Date/Context,  Source/Evidence. For example, an entry could be:
“David Hunter – American Values First PAC – Independent mailer support (unreported on candidate CF) –
Feb 2024 .” Another entry: “David Hunter – Appeared at Nancy Hart R.W. PAC event with County Judge
– Nov 2024 (photo on Facebook).” Such structured data can feed into Google Sheets for filtering
(e.g. filter all officials who share a donor) and into Looker Studio for visual dashboards (such as a
network graph of officials to donors/PACs). Highlight each flagged item (e.g. using a red font or icon
in the sheet) where a potential conflict or ethical concern is identified – for instance, any instance of
an official seen in a photo with a controversial figure, or any donor who does business with the
county.
By implementing these advanced search and audit techniques, Liberty County can systematically monitor
its elected officials. In the case of Constable David Hunter , our investigation so far flags the external PAC-
funded campaign mailers and his social ties with local political patrons (party PACs and a major developer)
as points warranting transparency. Continuing to populate a central database with such findings will enable
ongoing oversight and help safeguard public integrity across all offices.
