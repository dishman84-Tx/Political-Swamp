EXTERNAL LITIGATION AUDIT: CONFIRMED VIDEO EVIDENCE & CERTIFICATION FAILURES
Case Reference: The State of Texas v. Kelly Lynne Dishman Incident Date: December 14, 2025 | Agency: Montgomery County Sheriff’s Office (MCSO) Subject Officer: Deputy J. Murphy | Evidence Review: Axon Body 3 & Fleet Video (Confirmed)
I. FORENSIC VIDEO CONFIRMATION: THE "PINNED" VEHICLE SEIZURE
Evidence Source: Axon Fleet Video & Scene Photos (Drive Files). Audit Confirmation: Review of the digital evidence explicitly confirms that the Subject’s vehicle was "Pinned" (forcibly contained) by MCSO patrol units.
Tactical Containment: The video shows patrol vehicles positioned to physically trap the Subject's truck, rendering flight impossible. This maneuver classifies the stop as a high-level Use of Force/Seizure, not a routine traffic detention.
Causal Link to Damage: The activation of the "Service Engine" and "Collision Warning" lights (visible in the dash photos) correlates directly with the impact and stress of this containment maneuver.
Liability Finding: Deputy Murphy utilized this forcible technique against a driver who was already stationary and unconscious. This excessive use of force caused thousands of dollars in mechanical and structural damage to the vehicle, for which the agency is liable under the Texas Tort Claims Act.
II. AUDIO FORENSICS: PROOF OF MEDICAL INCAPACITATION
Evidence Source: Axon Body 3 Audio Track. Audit Confirmation: The audio recording captures the Subject "struggling to come to" and unable to form coherent responses.
Medical Mimicry: The audio signature is consistent with a "Post-Ictal" state (seizure aftermath) or "Involuntary Sedation" (drugging), distinct from the slur of voluntary alcohol intoxication.
Duty to Render Aid (TTC § 550.023): Deputy Murphy is recorded interacting with a citizen who is visibly and audibly incapable of basic motor function. His decision to proceed with standard DWI commands ("Get out of the car") instead of calling EMS is a documented failure to provide life-saving aid to a helpless individual.
Consent Validity: The audio proves the Subject was mentally incapacitated. Any "refusal" or "consent" recorded on the DIC-24 form is legally void, as the Subject lacked the cognitive capacity to understand the statutory warning.
III. OFFICER QUALIFICATIONS: TCOLE CERTIFICATION AUDIT
Subject: Deputy J. Murphy | Violation: Potential Lapse in Mandated Training. Statutory Standard: Texas Occupations Code § 1701 and TCOLE Rules mandate specific training cycles for peace officers.
Missing SFST Certification: There is no evidence in the provided file that Deputy Murphy possessed a current NHTSA Standardized Field Sobriety Test (SFST) Practitioner certificate at the time of the arrest. Without this specific, up-to-date credential, his administration of eye examinations (HGN) and balance tests is scientifically invalid.
Lapsed Training Hours: Under TCOLE Rule 217.11, officers must complete 40 hours of continuing education every 24-month training unit. A failure to maintain these hours results in the suspension of the officer's license by operation of law.
Brady List Implications: If Deputy Murphy performed a specialized DWI investigation without the valid underlying certification, his report constitutes a "False Government Record." This is grounds for immediate placement on the District Attorney’s Disclosure (Brady) List.
IV. ADMINISTRATIVE OBSTRUCTION: REFUSAL OF PUBLIC REQUESTS
Reference: Denial of Public Request for Bloodwork & Redacted "Axon" Files. Finding: MCSO has systematically refused to release the "Bloodwork Public Request Record" and unredacted "Axon" metadata, citing the Law Enforcement Exception (TPIA § 552.108). This denial is unlawful regarding the Subject’s own medical and digital records.
A. Refusal of Blood Information (Statutory Violation)
The agency refused to provide the toxicology results or the "Blood Public Request Report."
Violation of Texas Transportation Code § 724.018: This statute explicitly mandates that "On the request of a person who has given a specimen... full information concerning the analysis of the specimen shall be made available to the person."
Violation of Texas Health & Safety Code § 181 (Medical Records Privacy): The Subject, as the patient, has a right of access to her own biological data. The agency’s refusal to release the blood result prevents the Subject from proving Involuntary Intoxication (drugging), effectively suppressing the evidence of her innocence.
B. Suppression of "Axon" Metadata
The agency provided redacted files or refused "Axon" data related to the "Pin."
Hidden Evidence: The refusal to release the GPS and "Trigger" metadata from the Axon system conceals the speed and force used during the "Pinning" maneuver.
Right of Access (Gov't Code § 552.023): The Subject has a Special Right of Access to information that relates to her, including video of her own detention. Withholding the audio/video that proves the "pinned" status is an attempt to shield the department from liability for the vehicle damage.
V. PROPERTY & PROCEDURAL VIOLATIONS
Finding: Post-arrest handling of the vehicle and personal property violated standard inventory procedures.
Improper Tow/Secure: Following the "Pin," the vehicle was not properly secured. Personal identification papers were thrown onto the floorboard (as seen in photos), and a proper inventory list was not provided for the contents of the truck, only "personal items."
Spoliation of Evidence: By failing to document the "pinned" condition of the vehicle in the initial offense report, and then refusing to release the video showing the pin, the agency is engaging in the Spoliation of Evidence (destruction or hiding of proof).
